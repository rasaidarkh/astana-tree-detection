# Conclusion

This diploma project set out to design, implement and evaluate a complete software system capable of automatically detecting individual trees in satellite imagery of the city of Astana. The work was motivated by the practical need of *Zelenstroy* and other municipal services for a fast, repeatable and inexpensive alternative to the manual field survey, and by the explicit absence of any published deep-learning tree-detection benchmark on Central-Asian satellite imagery (Chapter 1).

## Achievement of the stated objectives

The six objectives of the introduction were addressed as follows.

**Objective 1 — state of the art.** Chapter 1 surveys 31 peer-reviewed publications of 2019 – 2025, organised by technical paradigm (two-stage detectors, one-stage detectors of the YOLO family, semantic-segmentation networks, the domain-specialised DeepForest model and the SAM / SAM 2 foundation models). The consolidated Table 1.2 establishes that fine-tuned models routinely achieve F1-scores in the 0.65 – 0.85 range on European and American urban data, and that the geographic-generalisation gap between an off-the-shelf and a fine-tuned configuration is in the order of 0.30 F-score points.

**Objective 2 — quantify the gap on Astana imagery.** Section 1.5 and Section 3.5.1 report the first measurement of the public NEON-pretrained DeepForest checkpoint on Astana satellite imagery: Box mAP@50 = **0.012** on the M14 validation set, one to two orders of magnitude below any fine-tuned configuration. This single number is the empirical anchor for the geographic-generalisation gap on Central Asia.

**Objective 3 — build a custom annotated Astana dataset.** Section 3.2 documents three iterative dataset batches (v1 / v2 / v3) constructed in CVAT through a model-in-the-loop pre-labelling workflow that reduced the per-image annotation cost from approximately 25 minutes (v1, from scratch) to approximately 4 minutes (v3, pre-labelled by the v2-finetune YOLO model). The final corpus is approximately 100 source images and ≈ 8 700 polygon annotations, and is, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the first of its kind for Astana.

**Objective 4 — train and combine four complementary models.** Chapters 2 and 3 document a structured **23-experiment hyperparameter ablation** of the YOLO branch spanning six orthogonal axes — model size, starting weights, optimiser, augmentation level, inference resolution and training-time data ordering — across five rounds (Section 3.3.7), a four-replicate variance check that places the variance band of the best single configuration at approximately ± 0.03 mAP (Section 3.3.9), and a random-split chain-learning control experiment that isolates inter-batch distribution drift as the dominant cause of degradation in continual-learning attempts (Section 3.3.10). Together with two Mask R-CNN checkpoints (v1+v2 base, v2+v3 fine-tune), the DeepForest v3 fine-tune chained from the deprecated v4 Roboflow checkpoint, and the SAM 2 zero-shot mask-refinement stage, all branches were evaluated on a single dedicated 14-image cross-model validation set (M14, 702 polygon annotations) — the methodological centrepiece of the diploma project — and the results are summarised in Section 3.7. The best single configuration is the **YOLOv8x-seg v4 production model** (`weights/v4_clean/v4_x_clean_v3val0.313_mergedval0.315.pt`) at Box mAP@50 = **0.315** and Mask mAP@50 = **0.289** on M14, a +140 % relative improvement over the YOLO v1 baseline. The DeepForest + SAM 2 pipeline (Box 0.146 / Mask 0.134) and the Mask R-CNN v2+v3 fine-tune (Box 0.166 / Mask 0.158) follow as the second and third best configurations. On the out-of-distribution v3-only subset (5 source images / 7 tiles / 497 polygons of previously-unseen Astana districts) the production model achieves Box mAP@50 = 0.313 — a 3.9× improvement over the v2-finetune baseline of 0.081 on the same subset, demonstrating that the pipeline is capable of efficient domain transfer to new districts with as few as 24 newly-annotated source images.

**Objective 5 — end-to-end software pipeline.** Chapter 2 describes the deployed system: a FastAPI Python backend with a pluggable four-model adapter interface (eight YOLO checkpoint variants exposed through a hierarchical model picker, plus DeepForest, DeepForest + SAM 2, Mask R-CNN, the YOLO + DeepForest Weighted-Box-Fusion ensemble and the cross-YOLO 4-way vote-based ensemble), a React 18 + Leaflet frontend with dark-mode-default styling and a centred per-action model picker, a four-mode geographic-conversion module supporting GeoTIFFs and informal screenshots alike, an in-browser map-capture feature that stitches ESRI or Google Satellite tiles for an arbitrary user-drawn rectangle or polygon (Auto-Zoom Region Scan with streaming NDJSON progress), SQLite persistent storage of every snapshot / run / detection ever produced by the system, and three export formats (GeoJSON, CSV, standalone HTML) that integrate the inventory with the user's existing GIS workflow.

**Objective 6 — evaluation on realistic Astana scenes.** Section 3.8 reports the end-to-end behaviour of the integrated pipeline: a 1 km × 1 km area captured at zoom 19 is processed in approximately 18 seconds on a single laptop GPU, comfortably below the 30-second budget set by the requirements. Qualitative cross-checkpoint inspection on Astana validation tiles confirms that the production v4 checkpoint produces an inventory that is qualitatively informative for the *Zelenstroy* end user, and that meaningful per-detection complementarity exists between the eight YOLO variants — motivating both the cross-YOLO voting ensemble (Section 3.7.5) and the broader methodological discussion of aggregate-mAP limitations in Section 3.9.

## Scientific and engineering contributions

1. **The first measured magnitude of the geographic-generalisation gap on Central-Asian satellite imagery** — Box mAP@50 = 0.012 for off-the-shelf NEON DeepForest on Astana, three orders of magnitude below the same model's reported off-the-shelf F-score on NAIP USA imagery.

2. **A single cross-model validation set (M14) and the first apples-to-apples ablation** of four neural-network families (YOLOv8-seg, Mask R-CNN, fine-tuned DeepForest, SAM 2 zero-shot mask refinement) on the same Astana ground truth. Section 3.7 reports the canonical Table 3.5 of the project.

3. **A 23-experiment hyperparameter ablation of the YOLO branch** spanning model size, starting weights, optimiser, augmentation level, inference resolution and training-time data ordering. The ablation establishes the counter-intuitive empirical finding that **the Ultralytics default augmentation pipeline outperforms our manually-tuned configurations** on Astana satellite imagery, that **model size has a U-shaped relationship with performance** on small annotated datasets (the medium-size yolov8m-seg at 27 M parameters and the large yolov8x-seg at 71 M with defaults both occupy local optima), and that **chain learning across version-batch boundaries hurts** by approximately 0.10 mAP relative to single-shot training, with the damage localised to inter-batch distribution drift rather than the staging mechanism itself (a random-split chain control gave 0.287 vs single-shot 0.308, while a version-split chain gave only 0.210). To the best of the authors' knowledge this is the first explicit hyperparameter ablation of this scale on Central-Asian satellite-tree data.

4. **A multi-replicate variance estimate of the best single YOLO configuration** — four independent training runs of the same exp1 configuration produced merged-val Box mAP@50 = 0.308 / 0.268 / 0.269 / 0.239 with sample mean 0.271 and standard deviation 0.028. This places the headline variance band of any single-shot YOLO experiment on this dataset at approximately ± 0.03 Box mAP@50, a methodological observation important for both the present project and any successor that relies on single-seed runs for model selection.

5. **A novel cross-YOLO vote-based ensemble** implemented in `backend/models/yolo_ensemble_adapter.py` and exposed in the frontend's hierarchical model picker under the "Ensemble → 4× YOLO vote" option. Member set is v4_x_clean + exp1_m_cocostart + v4_s_clean + v2-finetune; predictions are pooled, clustered by box-IoU at threshold 0.5 and retained only when at least two distinct member models agreed on the cluster. Qualitative evaluation on a representative tennis-court tile produces 3 000 raw pooled detections that vote-2 merges into 790 unified detections — a strong false-positive killer for surface types absent from training (notably the stadium-roof regression of the single-checkpoint v3-finetune-run1).

6. **An unexpected secondary finding**: the deprecated Roboflow-trained DeepForest v4 checkpoint scores Box mAP@50 = 0.004 on the M14 set — *below* the NEON pretrained baseline — because of an annotation-policy mismatch between the Roboflow bounding-box convention and the CVAT polygon convention. The result demonstrates that intra-Astana annotation-policy mismatch can dominate the floristic gap between continents.

7. **A reusable Astana satellite-tree dataset** of approximately 100 source images and ≈ 8 700 polygon annotations across three iterative batches, with a documented annotation-cost reduction from 25 minutes per image (v1, from scratch) to 4 minutes per image (v3, model-in-the-loop pre-labelling).

8. **A deployable software prototype** that turns the trained models into a usable internal tool: pluggable adapter interface, four geographic-conversion modes, SQLite persistence, polygon and rectangle scan workflows, three exporters, dark-mode-default UI with a centred per-action model picker, and a city-map aggregate view that grows organically as the user processes new districts.

## Limitations and future work

The present implementation has the limitations listed in Section 3.9: a cross-model validation set of only 14 source images and 702 polygons, a single class label that does not distinguish species, no explicit shadow modelling, an inference-time SAM 2 mask cost of approximately 1.5 seconds per image, no quantitative M14 evaluation of either ensemble (WBF or cross-YOLO vote), a measured stadium-roof false-positive regression on the single-checkpoint v3 production model that the cross-YOLO vote ensemble mitigates qualitatively but is not yet quantified, and a single-laptop deployment.

A central methodological observation that surfaces from the empirical work and that the thesis discusses in Section 3.9 is that **aggregate metrics such as mAP@50 are insufficient as a single criterion for production model selection on small datasets**. Cross-checkpoint visual comparison on the same Astana input scene shows that different models with statistically indistinguishable aggregate mAP can detect substantially different per-detection subsets (we measure a 132-tree spread on a single 1 236 × 1 159 pixel tile between the most conservative checkpoint at 687 detections and the most permissive at 819) — a known phenomenon in detection-model evaluation that motivates the ensemble strategies implemented in this work but rarely surfaces in the published satellite-tree literature.

The natural directions for future work are: (i) **dataset expansion to 200 – 500 source images** with the explicit goal of reaching the F1 = 0.7 range demonstrated on European urban data, in particular adding tiles of built-environment scenes (stadiums, industrial zones, large impervious surfaces) that produce the dominant false-positive failure mode of the current production checkpoint; (ii) **quantitative M14 evaluation and per-class weight calibration** of the WBF ensemble and the cross-YOLO vote ensemble; (iii) **species-level annotation and a multi-class detection head**, since species-specific pruning and replacement is a separate budget line in the municipal urban-forestry plan; (iv) **OpenStreetMap building-footprint post-filtering** at inference time, dropping any predicted box whose centroid falls inside a tagged building polygon — a zero-training-cost mitigation of the stadium-roof regression that would not require any dataset expansion; (v) **SAM 2 video propagation** for multi-temporal monitoring of canopy change across successive satellite acquisitions; (vi) **Sentinel-2 city-wide canopy-cover monitoring** as a complement to the very-high-resolution per-tree inventory; and (vii) **multi-user containerised deployment** with authentication and per-organisation isolation so the system can be adopted as part of *Zelenstroy*'s day-to-day operations.

## Closing remark

This work demonstrates that deep-learning techniques developed for tree detection in American forests, European cities and Asian metropolises can be adapted to the very different geographic, architectural and floristic context of a Central-Asian capital through a relatively modest annotation effort and an off-the-shelf software stack. Beyond the headline Box mAP@50 of 0.315, the project contributes a systematic empirical methodology — single cross-model M14 validation set, four-replicate variance estimation, six-axis hyperparameter ablation, qualitative cross-checkpoint complementarity analysis — that can be reused by any successor project on Astana or any analogous Central-Asian city. The resulting system delivers, for the first time, a quantitative baseline for automated urban-tree detection in Astana, a deployable prototype that can be operated by a non-technical user from a web browser, and a reusable template for future work in the region.
