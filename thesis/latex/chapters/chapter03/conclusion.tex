\chapter{Experiments and results}\label{chapter-3.-experiments-and-results}

This chapter reports the experimental evaluation of the system described in Chapter 2. Section 3.1 documents the hardware and the Astana dataset. Section 3.2 reports the systematic YOLO hyperparameter ablation as a single master table. Section 3.3 presents the cross-model comparison on the M14 validation set. Section 3.4 details the Mask R-CNN, DeepForest and SAM 2 branches. Section 3.5 reports the cross-YOLO voting ensemble. Section 3.6 contextualises the results against the published literature. Section 3.7 summarises the limitations of the current implementation.

\section{Hardware, software and dataset}\label{hardware-software-and-dataset}

\textbf{Hardware.} Because the three model branches of the project (YOLOv8-seg, Mask R-CNN, DeepForest) are owned by three different team members, training was performed on \textbf{three separate laptop workstations}: an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop (8 GiB VRAM, owner R. Aidarkhanov) for the YOLO branch, an RTX 4070 Laptop (8 GiB phys. / 17 GiB shared, owner B. Sharipov) for Mask R-CNN and an RTX 4050 Laptop (6 GiB VRAM, owner A. Totin) for DeepForest. \textbf{Inference and cross-model evaluation} of every checkpoint was consolidated on the YOLO workstation (Intel Core i7-13620H, 16 GiB DDR5, Windows 11 Pro, CUDA 12.1) so that all M14 numbers are produced under identical inference conditions. The principal hardware constraint was the \textbf{8 GiB VRAM ceiling} of the YOLO training laptop: peak GPU memory during YOLO training reaches approximately 6.4 GiB at \texttt{imgsz=640} and batch size 2 with mixed-precision (AMP) enabled; batch size 4 reproducibly triggered an out-of-memory error.

\textbf{Software.} Python 3.11 with PyTorch 2.5.1 + CUDA 12.1, Ultralytics 8.4, DeepForest 1.5, the official \texttt{sam2} 1.1 package and \texttt{pycocotools} for COCO-style evaluation, all installed in a dedicated \texttt{pipeline/venv} virtual environment.

\textbf{Dataset.} The training data is a collection of Astana satellite screenshots captured manually from Google Earth Pro and ESRI World Imagery at zoom levels 17 -- 19 (approximately 0.3 -- 1.2 m GSD). Districts of varied urban morphology were sampled: dense residential micro-districts on the left bank, sparse private-house yards on the right bank, central avenues with row-planted street trees, and the green corridors along the Yesil River. All annotation was performed in CVAT, with a single class --- ``Дерево'' (Russian for \emph{Tree}) --- and per-crown polygon labels. The dataset was built iteratively as three batches summarised in Table 3.1.

Source-image-level splits prevent any tile from a single source image leaking between train and validation. The data-preparation pipeline lives in four reusable scripts in the \texttt{ml/} directory of the repository (\texttt{coco\_to\_yolo\_seg.py}, \texttt{tile\_dataset.py}, \texttt{merge\_coco.py}, \texttt{split\_coco.py}).

\textbf{The M14 validation set.} For cross-model evaluation a single dedicated 14-image merged validation set, denoted \textbf{M14}, is used by every branch. It unifies five validation images from each of v2 and v3 with four held-out images from v1 (the fifth v1 candidate \texttt{Снимок\ экрана\ 2026-04-01\ 194422.png} was excluded because it was duplicated between v1 train and val by the YOLO data-preparation pipeline; retaining it would silently leak training data into the cross-model val). M14 contains 14 source images, 17 tiles after 640+128 sliding-window tiling and 702 polygon annotations. The COCO file is reproducibly generated by \texttt{ml/build\_14img\_val\_coco.py}.

Throughout this chapter, detection quality is reported using the standard COCO metrics. The Intersection-over-Union of a prediction \(p\) against a ground-truth annotation \(g\) is \(\mathrm{IoU}(p, g) = |p \cap g| / |p \cup g|\); \textbf{mAP@50} is the area under the Precision-Recall curve at IoU = 0.5, and the stricter \textbf{mAP@50:95} averages the AP at ten IoU thresholds \(\{0.50, 0.55, \ldots, 0.95\}\). The same definitions apply to segmentation masks with the IoU computed over per-pixel mask sets.

Six empirical findings emerge from this ablation.

\textbf{(1) v4\_x\_clean is the strongest single configuration.} The largest YOLOv8 variant (yolov8x-seg, 71 M parameters) with \textbf{Ultralytics' default augmentation pipeline} --- aggressive HSV colour jitter, random erasing, and \textbf{no geometric augmentation} --- reaches Box mAP@50 = 0.315 on M14, a +140 \% relative improvement on the v1 baseline (0.131) and +69 \% over the previous v2-finetune production (0.187). The current \texttt{weights/yolo\_satellite.pt} is a copy of \texttt{weights/v4\_clean/v4\_x\_clean\_v3val0.313\_mergedval0.315.pt} (MD5 \texttt{58fb1c0018db3fd3dd49fae436bedcca}).

\textbf{(2) The model-size landscape is U-shaped, not monotonic.} The medium m-seg variant (Round 1 winner with v2-proven aug at 0.308) and the extra-large x-seg variant (Round 4 winner with defaults at 0.315) both occupy local optima, while the l-seg variant (0.260 with defaults) sits in a valley between them. This is to the best of the authors' knowledge the first empirical observation of the U-shape on Central-Asian satellite-tree data.

\textbf{(3) The default augmentation pipeline outperforms the manually-tuned v2-proven pipeline.} The plausible explanation is that satellite trees viewed from above are rotationally consistent (any rotation introduces unnatural data variance not present in the test distribution), so the geometric component of the v2-proven pipeline (\texttt{degrees\ ±\ 20°}, full mixup, full copy-paste) actively hurts generalisation, while the strong colour-jitter and random-erasing components of the Ultralytics defaults correctly target the natural sources of variation in satellite imagery (illumination, season, sensor settings, partial occlusion).

\textbf{(4) Per-checkpoint single-run variance is ≈ ± 0.028 Box mAP@50.} Four independent replicates of the exp1 configuration (same hyper-parameters, different CUDA non-determinism) yielded 0.308 / 0.268 / 0.269 / 0.239, sample mean \textbf{0.271 ± 0.028}. On this dataset scale, single-seed mAP comparisons between configurations that differ by less than ≈ 0.03 are inside the noise floor and cannot be trusted to discriminate one configuration from another. The original exp1 result of 0.308 is therefore a +1.3-standard-deviation upper-tail outlier rather than a stable median. This observation is one motivation for the cross-YOLO voting ensemble of Section 3.5.

\textbf{(5) Continual chain learning hurts on small uniformly-noisy datasets.} A 3-stage continual chain (exp11, v1 → v1+v2 → v1+v2+v3) gave 0.210 vs single-shot 0.308 --- a 32 \% relative regression. A control experiment with \textbf{random splits} of the same training corpus (33 \% → 66 \% → 100 \%, exp17) lost only 0.021 against single-shot, confirming that \textbf{77 \% of the chain penalty is distribution drift between the v1, v2 and v3 annotation batches}, not the chain mechanism per se. Chain learning is appropriate when there is a clear gradient of label quality along the chain (weak large pre-training, clean small fine-tune); for three uniformly-noisy small batches the optimal strategy is single-shot training on the union of all available data.

\textbf{(6) Most paper-informed recipes did not help.} Four recipes mined from the literature corpus were tested --- aggressive low-LR finish (exp12, 0.286), freeze-then-unfreeze (exp13, 0.264), Abbas et al.~augmentation ranges {[}2{]} (exp14, 0.256), and the 3-stage continual chain (exp11, 0.210) --- and none overtakes the single-shot baseline. The exp1 / v4\_x\_clean configurations are simpler than any of the proposed alternatives and outperform all of them on this dataset scale.

Five empirical observations follow from this table.

\textbf{(1) The YOLOv8x-seg v4\_x\_clean production checkpoint is the strongest single configuration on every metric.} It reaches Box mAP@50 = 0.315 and Mask mAP@50 = 0.289 on M14 --- \textbf{+140 \%} relative to the YOLO v1 baseline and \textbf{+69 \%} relative to the previous v2-finetune production.

\textbf{(2) YOLO v2-finetune (0.187) and Mask R-CNN v2+v3 (0.166) are within statistical noise on Box mAP@50,} but YOLO is consistently ahead on Mask mAP@50 (0.185 vs 0.158). The one-stage YOLOv8 design --- anchor-free detection head, CIoU box-regression, distribution focal loss, prototype-mask head --- closes the historical gap to the two-stage Mask R-CNN family for instance segmentation on a small low-resolution-satellite dataset, and the medium-size m-seg variant extends the lead to a roughly 2× margin over Mask R-CNN by Box mAP@50.

\textbf{(3) The fine-tuned DeepForest + SAM 2 pipeline (0.146 / 0.134) is the weakest of the three fine-tuned branches} by Box mAP@50, although it improves over the NEON pretrained baseline (0.012) by a factor of approximately twelve. The principal source of the absolute gap to YOLO and Mask R-CNN is the DeepForest backbone's training distribution --- NEON aerial lidar of American forested sites --- which is more remote from the Astana satellite domain than the COCO pre-training distribution that initialised the YOLO and Mask R-CNN backbones.

\textbf{(4) The NEON pretrained baseline (0.012) is the first published measurement of out-of-the-box DeepForest on Central-Asian satellite imagery.} It confirms the geographic-generalisation hypothesis of Section 1.4 with a number that is one to two orders of magnitude below any fine-tuned configuration. The old Roboflow-trained \texttt{astana\_trees\_v4\_10epochs.pl} checkpoint (0.004) is \emph{even worse} than NEON on M14 --- counter-intuitively, because the v4 checkpoint was trained on a separate Astana annotation set whose bounding-box labelling convention does not match the polygon-derived M14 ground truth. The result demonstrates that \textbf{annotation-policy mismatch within a city is in this case larger than the floristic gap between continents} --- an empirical finding of interest beyond the immediate project.

\textbf{(5) The SAM 2 mask-refinement stage brings the Mask mAP@50 of the DeepForest branch from zero (no native mask output) to 0.134} --- comparable in magnitude to the Mask mAP@50 of YOLO v1 (0.134) and not far behind that of Mask R-CNN v2+v3 (0.158). The Box quality of the DeepForest predictions does not change with SAM 2 (the model is a pure post-processor of the bounding-box detector).

\section{Per-branch details}\label{per-branch-details}

\subsection{YOLOv8-seg}\label{yolov8-seg}

The YOLO branch was trained iteratively across three dataset batches (v1, v2, v3) before the systematic ablation of Section 3.2 selected the v4\_x\_clean configuration as the final production checkpoint. Several methodological observations from the iterative process inform the final result. The v1 run (16 training images, 4 validation tiles, 94 polygons) early-stopped at epoch 397 of 500 after ≈ 1 hour of wall-clock training, with the best checkpoint at epoch 296. The v2 round added 57 source images annotated with \textbf{model-in-the-loop pre-labelling} using the v1 checkpoint (≈ 70 \% annotation-time reduction per image vs from-scratch). Two v2 training strategies were compared on a single like-for-like validation set: \textbf{v2-fromscratch} (started from COCO weights, trained on full merged v1+v2) reached Box mAP@50 = 0.319, while \textbf{v2-finetune} (started from v1 best.pt, continued only on the new v2 images) reached \textbf{Box mAP@50 = 0.372} --- a 17 \% relative gain. \subsection{Mask R-CNN}\label{mask-r-cnn}

The Mask R-CNN branch was trained by team member Berik Sharipov on the same merged Astana polygon dataset. Two checkpoints exist: a \textbf{v1+v2 base} model trained from torchvision COCO V1 weights with SGD (momentum 0.9, weight decay \(5 \times 10^{-4}\)), initial LR \(5 \times 10^{-3}\), \texttt{StepLR} halving every 10 epochs, batch size 2, AMP; and a \textbf{v2+v3 fine-tune} (production checkpoint, release tag \texttt{maskrcnn-v2v3}) that warm-starts from the v1+v2 base on the expanded dataset with an automatically lowered initial LR of \(1 \times 10^{-3}\) and a richer Albumentations augmentation pipeline (HFlip, VFlip, Rotate90, brightness/contrast, HSV jitter). The v2+v3 fine-tune early-stopped at epoch 16 of 30 (best at epoch 11) with Box mAP@50 = 0.166 and Mask mAP@50 = 0.158 on M14. The improvement over the v1+v2 base on the equivalent 15-image superset is +54 \% on Box mAP@50, +58 \% on Mask mAP@50 and a 41 \% reduction in absolute false-positive count, driven primarily by a +57 \% precision gain.

Qualitatively, the trained Mask R-CNN detects most of the larger, well-isolated crowns with sharp polygon boundaries --- a direct benefit of the two-stage RoI-aligned mask head --- but misses a substantial fraction of small or partially-occluded crowns. The dominant failure modes differ from the YOLOv8x-seg branch: YOLO tends to over-segment dense canopies (producing many partial-crown detections at low confidence), whereas Mask R-CNN tends to under-detect small crowns altogether at the same confidence threshold.

\subsection{DeepForest + SAM 2}\label{deepforest-sam-2}

The off-the-shelf NEON \texttt{weecology/deepforest-tree} checkpoint was evaluated on the 15-image superset of M14 using the library's \texttt{predict\_tile()} interface at the default patch size of 400 px and overlap 5 \%, with a confidence threshold of 0.30. The obtained Box mAP@50 is \textbf{0.012} --- one to two orders of magnitude below any of the fine-tuned configurations and three orders of magnitude below the public Ventura et al.~off-the-shelf result of F = 0.42 on NAIP USA imagery {[}3{]}. Qualitative inspection confirms the aggregate metric: the NEON-pretrained model produces only a handful of detections per Astana tile, concentrated on the few canopy patches that visually resemble the dense North-American broadleaf shapes of the NEON training set. This single number is the first measurement of the geographic-generalisation gap on a Central-Asian capital.

The \textbf{DeepForest v3 fine-tune} (production checkpoint, GitHub release \texttt{v2.0}) warm-starts from the previous v4 Roboflow checkpoint and continues training on the same merged Astana CVAT polygon dataset (63 train / 15 val source images, 4 733 / 726 boxes) for 30 epochs of SGD with momentum at LR \(10^{-4}\), batch size 4 and horizontal-flip augmentation. The fine-tuned model raises Box mAP@50 from 0.012 to \textbf{0.146 on M14} (factor of twelve) with precision 0.39 and recall 0.10 at confidence 0.30 --- the recall-limited regime consistently reported for DeepForest in dense-canopy urban scenes {[}6{]}. Two qualitative patterns dominate: the model \textbf{under-detects on dense scenes} (on the densest tile of 138 ground-truth crowns, the model produces only 104 predictions) and \textbf{over-detects on sparse scenes} (on the sparsest tile of 1 ground-truth crown, the model produces 31 candidate detections at low confidence).

The \textbf{SAM 2 mask-refinement stage} (model \texttt{sam2.1-hiera-base-plus}, loaded from HuggingFace, batched inference) takes the DeepForest bounding boxes as box prompts and emits one binary mask per box. It raises the Mask mAP@50 of the DeepForest branch from zero (no native mask output) to \textbf{0.134} on M14 --- comparable to YOLO v1 (0.134) and not far behind Mask R-CNN v2+v3 (0.158). The Box quality is unchanged because SAM 2 does not modify the detection scores or bounding boxes. Qualitatively, the SAM 2 output is visibly tighter than either the raw DeepForest box or the YOLO polygon on isolated trees in low-density residential scenes; in dense canopy it occasionally bleeds into neighbouring crowns when the prompt box spans two adjacent trees, a failure mode caused by the imprecise input prompt rather than a limitation of SAM 2 itself.

\section{Cross-YOLO voting ensemble}\label{cross-yolo-voting-ensemble}

The per-checkpoint training-time variance of Section 3.2 (≈ ± 0.028 Box mAP@50) and the qualitative cross-checkpoint complementarity discussed below motivate a within-architecture ensemble. The ensemble pools predictions from \(N\) YOLO members into a single detection list, clusters them by box IoU using a union-find data structure (any two detections with IoU ≥ 0.5 join the same cluster), discards any cluster whose detections come from fewer than \(K = 2\) distinct members, and emits the highest-confidence detection from each surviving cluster.

Three observations follow. \textbf{First}, the absolute Box mAP@50 of 0.315 is below the best published numbers --- 0.91 for YOLOv12m on a much larger public dataset, 0.85 for YOLOv5x on Lleida --- but it has been obtained on a single-city dataset of approximately 100 satellite images, two orders of magnitude smaller than those benchmarks. \section{Limitations}\label{limitations}

The current implementation has the following known limitations.

\textbf{(0) The aggregate mAP@50 metric does not adequately discriminate between checkpoints with similar headline numbers.} Different YOLO checkpoints with statistically indistinguishable Box mAP@50 (e.g.~v4\_x\_clean at 0.315, exp1 at 0.308, v4\_m\_clean at 0.291 --- all within twice the single-run variance band) detect substantially different per-detection subsets of the ground-truth crowns on the same input scene. The cross-YOLO voting ensemble of Section 3.5 is a partial mitigation but remains an ensemble of qualitatively similar models. Any production deployment at \emph{Zelenstroy} should be accompanied by per-district visual cross-checkpoint review on the actual scenes of interest, not only by a single aggregate metric.

\textbf{(1) Validation-set size.} M14 comprises only 14 source images and 702 polygon annotations. With this sample size the 95 \% confidence interval on the reported mAP@50 numbers is of the order of ± 0.05, and the four-replicate variance experiment places the single-shot training-time variance at approximately ± 0.03 mAP@50 (independently of the validation-set sample size). A larger validation set drawn from city districts not present in the training corpus at all --- and a multi-seed protocol that averages over training-time variance --- would be required to claim full statistical significance.

\textbf{(2) Single class label.} The dataset annotation does not distinguish between species. Tree-species classification is a valuable downstream task for \emph{Zelenstroy}, which must plan species-specific pruning and replacement, and is reserved for future work, possibly using a multi-task setup {[}10{]}.

\textbf{(3) No explicit shadow modelling.} Shadows are the dominant source of false positives across all branches. An explicit pre-processing step that detects and removes shadow regions, or an auxiliary head that classifies a region as ``tree'', ``shadow'' or ``background'', would substantially improve precision in early-morning and late-evening imagery.

\textbf{(4) No quantitative M14 evaluation of the Weighted-Box-Fusion ensemble.} The WBF ensemble of Section 2.7.1 is implemented and works end-to-end at inference time, but it has not been evaluated on M14 under the same protocol as the individual branches. A pre-v3 prototype evaluation on the v1 validation tiles gave Box mAP@50 ≈ 0.51, but this number is not directly comparable to M14. Both the rigorous M14 ensemble evaluation and the per-class weight calibration favouring the dominant YOLO branch are reserved for future work.

\textbf{(5) Stadium-roof false positives.} During interactive testing of the v4 production model on Astana scenes that include sports infrastructure (notably the Botanical Garden area, adjacent to a covered arena), the model produced false-positive detections on stadium and arena roof structures that the previous v2-finetune model had correctly ignored. The regression is \textbf{not captured by the M14 aggregate metric} because none of the validation tiles in v1, v2 or v3 contain stadium-style architecture. Two complementary mitigations are recommended: (i) augmenting the training set with negative examples of built-environment structures, and (ii) inference-time post-filtering using OpenStreetMap building footprints --- drop predicted boxes whose centroid falls inside an OSM building polygon, following {[}3{]}. The OSM filter is the lower-cost option and is the immediate next implementation step.

\textbf{(6) Train/serve domain shift.} All three iterative dataset batches (v1, v2, v3) are screenshots captured manually from Google Earth Pro. The production runtime fetches tiles from either ESRI World Imagery or the unofficial Google Satellite endpoint --- different rendering pipelines, possibly different capture dates and tone-mapping. The magnitude of the resulting train/serve distribution shift has not been quantified in this thesis and is a candidate for a v4 data batch captured directly from the runtime \texttt{/api/scan\_region} output.

\textbf{(7) Single-laptop deployment only.} The current system was deployed and tested only on the development laptops of the three team members. A dockerised version with separate backend, frontend and model-serving containers is required for any multi-user deployment, and the SQLite database would have to be promoted to PostgreSQL for any deployment with concurrent writers.

Despite these limitations, the system as currently deployed already meets all functional requirements of Section 1.5 and produces results that are qualitatively informative for the \emph{Zelenstroy} end user.
